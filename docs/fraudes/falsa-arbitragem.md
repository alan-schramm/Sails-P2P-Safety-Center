# Fake support, impersonation, and arbitration pressure

**Scenario:** someone claims to be a platform employee, moderator, or arbiter and asks you to move the conversation, reveal secrets, send an extra payment, or accept an outcome outside the authorized dispute process.

## Warning signs
- A supposed support agent contacts you through an unsolicited private message.
- You are asked to disclose a recovery phrase, private key, authentication code, or unrelated wallet signature.
- Someone claims an off-platform transfer is required to unlock escrow or accelerate arbitration.
- A ruling is presented only as a screenshot or chat message, without verification in the application's authorized dispute interface.

## What to do
1. Access support or disputes using the official application interface or a previously verified address.
2. Check the trade identifier and dispute status in the authorized workflow.
3. Do not send extra funds or secrets to someone claiming special authority.
4. Preserve the conversation and report suspected impersonation through the relevant official channel.

## Limits
Arbitration models vary by implementation. An arbiter's role, selection, appeal rights, and authority must be verified against the actual application's policies. A claimed ruling is not proof of a valid settlement instruction.

**Claim status:** general guidance. This page does not assert that any particular arbitration mode, appeal, or fraud detector is enabled in a given deployment.
