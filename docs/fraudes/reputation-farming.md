# Reputation farming and exit scams

**Scenario:** a trader builds a reassuring history with small transactions, then proposes a much larger or unusual transaction that exposes the counterparty to greater loss.

## Warning signs
- Many small, repetitive trades followed by a sudden request for a much larger amount.
- An account's positive history is concentrated among a few counterparties.
- Pressure to bypass limits, change payment instructions, or transact outside the agreed flow.

## What to do
1. Evaluate the specific trade amount, payment method, account details, and settlement risk, not just a reputation score.
2. Keep the transaction within the applicable platform limits and documented protections.
3. Treat unusually favorable terms and requests for exceptions as reasons to verify, not proof of fraud.
4. Pause when the trade's risk differs materially from the history you relied on.

## Limits
Reputation is a risk signal, not an identity guarantee. Colluding accounts may manufacture apparent activity, and a legitimate account may later be compromised. Neither high trade volume nor a signed payment account eliminates loss risk.

**Claim status:** general safety guidance. The protocol's anti-Sybil graph analysis, time decay, and counterparty-diversity scoring are described as planned, not universally deployed controls.
