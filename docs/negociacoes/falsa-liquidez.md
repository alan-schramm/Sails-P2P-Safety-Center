# Fake liquidity and offers that look too good

**Scenario:** an attractive P2P offer promises unusually favorable pricing or large available volume but has limited verifiable completion history.

## Warning signs
- Prices are far outside comparable offers without a clear explanation.
- Large advertised volume with little independently verifiable trading history.
- Requests to pay upfront, bypass escrow, or move settlement to an unrelated channel.

## What to do
1. Compare terms, limits, fees, and payment method with other available offers.
2. Check the trader's relevant history without treating ratings as a guarantee.
3. Confirm that the selected application's actual settlement flow provides the protections it advertises.
4. Do not transfer funds outside the agreed process because of a supposedly limited-time offer.

## Limits
An offer's appearance does not prove available liquidity, and a platform may have no independent way to verify every claim. Trust scores and transaction caps are not substitutes for settlement verification.

**Claim status:** general safety guidance. The Sails Protocol threat registry identifies fake liquidity as a high-severity threat; QVAC-based offer scoring is not a universally implemented safeguard.
